How long must you keep business records in South Africa?

SARS expects records to be kept for five years in most cases. What to keep, how long, and how to store it safely.

Banda Bookkeepers · Updated 21 November 2026

Good bookkeeping is not only about getting this month right. It is also about being able to produce the records behind a return years later, if SARS asks.

The general rule: five years

Under the Tax Administration Act, a person who has submitted a return must keep the records, books of account and documents for five years from the date the return was submitted. A person who should have submitted a return but did not must keep records until it is submitted. Records relevant to an audit, investigation, objection or appeal must be kept until that matter is finalised. Source: SARS — Record keeping (checked 21 November 2026).

Employers must also keep employee tax records for at least five years following the submission of the related return (SARS Guide for Employers in respect of Employees' Tax).

What to keep

  • Bank, card and loan statements
  • Sales invoices, credit notes and receipts issued
  • Supplier tax invoices and receipts
  • Payroll records: payslips, registers, leave and employee tax certificates
  • Asset purchase and sale documents
  • Contracts, leases and loan agreements
  • Your general ledger, reconciliations and year-end working papers

Keeping records safely

SARS accepts records in their original form or in an acceptable electronic form. A simple, consistent digital filing system — one folder per month, scanned slips named by date and supplier, statements stored as PDFs — makes records easy to find and protects against loss. Because records contain personal information, store them securely and in line with POPIA.

This guide is general information, not tax advice for your situation. For advice on a specific matter, speak to a registered tax practitioner at Think Mybiz Accountants.
