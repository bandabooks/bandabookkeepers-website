What does a bookkeeper actually do each month?

A month-by-month look at the work behind clean books: capturing, reconciling, debtors, creditors, payroll records and the monthly report.

Banda Bookkeepers · Updated 21 November 2026

Bookkeeping is the routine work of recording every financial transaction in your business and checking that the records agree with reality. Done monthly, it keeps your numbers usable all year — not just at tax time.

1. Capturing and coding transactions

Every payment in and out of your bank accounts, credit cards and petty cash is recorded and allocated to the right account: sales, rent, fuel, stock, salaries, loan repayments and so on. Correct coding is what makes your income statement meaningful and your VAT figures defensible.

2. Reconciling bank and card accounts

A reconciliation compares the balance in your books with the balance on your bank statement and explains every difference. It catches duplicated entries, missing transactions, bank charges you did not expect and payments that were never recorded. An unreconciled account is the most common reason books cannot be trusted.

3. Keeping debtors and creditors up to date

Your debtors list shows who owes you money and for how long. Your creditors list shows what you owe suppliers and when it is due. Kept current, they protect your cash flow: you chase overdue invoices on time and avoid paying a supplier twice.

4. Payroll records

For employers, the bookkeeper keeps payslips, payroll journals, leave records and the monthly figures needed for employer returns, so salaries in your books match what was actually paid.

5. VAT-ready records

For VAT vendors, each transaction carries the right VAT treatment, and the bookkeeper prepares working papers that support the VAT return. When SARS asks for support, the invoices and reconciliations are already there.

6. The monthly report

At month-end you receive a short report: income, expenses, profit, cash position and anything that needs your input. This is where bookkeeping stops being admin and starts being useful for decisions.

What a bookkeeper does not do

Bookkeeping produces the records. Preparing annual financial statements, completing tax returns and giving tax advice is accounting and tax work. At our practice that work is done by our main firm, Think Mybiz Accountants and Tax Practitioners, using the books we keep. See bookkeeper vs accountant for where the line sits.
